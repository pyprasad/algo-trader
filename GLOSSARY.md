# Glossary of Terms

## Trading Terms

### A

**Ask (Offer)**
The price at which a seller is willing to sell. In a quote, this is the higher price.

**ATR (Average True Range)**
A technical indicator that measures market volatility by decomposing the entire range of an asset price for a period.

**Arbitrage**
The simultaneous purchase and sale of an asset to profit from a difference in price across different markets.

### B

**Backtesting**
The process of testing a trading strategy on historical data to evaluate its performance.

**Bear Market**
A market condition where prices are falling or expected to fall.

**Bid**
The price at which a buyer is willing to buy. In a quote, this is the lower price.

**Bollinger Bands**
A technical indicator consisting of a middle band (SMA) with two outer bands representing standard deviations.

**Bull Market**
A market condition where prices are rising or expected to rise.

### C

**CFD (Contract for Difference)**
A financial derivative that allows traders to speculate on price movements without owning the underlying asset.

**Circuit Breaker**
An automatic mechanism that halts trading when certain thresholds are breached.

**Correlation**
A statistical measure of how two securities move in relation to each other.

**CST (Client Session Token)**
Authentication token used for IG Markets API sessions.

### D

**Drawdown**
The peak-to-trough decline during a specific period for an investment or trading account.

**DFB (Daily Funded Bet)**
A type of spread bet that has no fixed expiry date and incurs daily funding charges.

### E

**EMA (Exponential Moving Average)**
A moving average that gives more weight to recent prices, making it more responsive to new information.

**Epic**
IG Markets' unique identifier for a tradeable market.

**Equity**
The total value of a trading account, including open positions.

**Execution**
The completion of a buy or sell order.

### F

**Fill**
The execution of an order at a specific price.

**Financing Cost**
The daily charge or credit for holding a position overnight.

**FOMC (Federal Open Market Committee)**
The branch of the Federal Reserve that determines monetary policy.

### G

**Gap**
A price range where no trading occurred, typically between closing and opening prices.

**Guaranteed Stop**
A stop loss order that guarantees execution at the specified price, regardless of market gaps.

### H

**Hedge**
A position taken to offset potential losses in another investment.

**High-Frequency Trading (HFT)**
Algorithmic trading characterized by high speeds, high turnover rates, and high order-to-trade ratios.

### I

**Indicator**
A mathematical calculation based on price and/or volume used to predict future price movements.

### K

**Kelly Criterion**
A mathematical formula for determining optimal position size based on win probability and payoff ratio.

### L

**Leverage**
The use of borrowed capital to increase potential returns (and risks).

**Limit Order**
An order to buy or sell at a specific price or better.

**Liquidity**
The degree to which an asset can be quickly bought or sold without affecting its price.

**Long Position**
A position that profits from rising prices (buying).

### M

**MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)**
A trend-following momentum indicator showing the relationship between two moving averages.

**Margin**
The amount of money required to open and maintain a leveraged position.

**Margin Call**
A broker's demand for additional funds when an account's margin falls below the required level.

**Market Order**
An order to buy or sell immediately at the current market price.

**Maximum Drawdown**
The largest peak-to-trough decline in account value.

### N

**NFP (Non-Farm Payrolls)**
A key US economic indicator reporting the number of jobs added or lost in the economy.

### O

**OCO (One-Cancels-Other)**
A pair of orders where execution of one automatically cancels the other.

**Order Book**
A list of buy and sell orders for a specific security organized by price level.

### P

**P&L (Profit and Loss)**
The gain or loss on a position or portfolio.

**Pip**
The smallest price move in a currency exchange rate (typically 0.0001 for major pairs).

**Position Sizing**
The process of determining how much capital to allocate to a trade.

### Q

**Quote**
The current bid and ask prices for a security.

### R

**RSI (Relative Strength Index)**
A momentum oscillator measuring the speed and magnitude of price changes.

**Risk/Reward Ratio**
The potential profit of a trade compared to its potential loss.

**Rollover**
The process of extending a position's settlement date, often incurring financing charges.

### S

**Scalping**
A trading strategy that attempts to profit from small price changes.

**Sharpe Ratio**
A measure of risk-adjusted return, calculated as excess return divided by standard deviation.

**Short Position**
A position that profits from falling prices (selling).

**Slippage**
The difference between expected and actual execution prices.

**SMA (Simple Moving Average)**
The average price over a specified number of periods.

**Spread**
The difference between bid and ask prices.

**Stop Loss**
An order to close a position when it reaches a certain loss level.

**Support/Resistance**
Price levels where a security tends to stop and reverse.

### T

**Take Profit**
An order to close a position when it reaches a certain profit level.

**Technical Analysis**
The study of past market data to forecast future price movements.

**Tick**
The minimum price movement of a trading instrument.

**Trailing Stop**
A stop loss that moves with favorable price movements but remains fixed during adverse movements.

### V

**VaR (Value at Risk)**
A statistical measure of potential portfolio loss over a specific time frame.

**Volatility**
The degree of variation in trading prices over time.

**Volume**
The number of shares or contracts traded in a security.

**VWAP (Volume Weighted Average Price)**
The average price weighted by volume, often used as a trading benchmark.

### W

**Win Rate**
The percentage of profitable trades out of total trades.

**Working Order**
A pending order that hasn't been executed yet.

## Technical Terms

### A

**API (Application Programming Interface)**
A set of protocols and tools for building software applications.

**Asynchronous**
Operations that don't block execution while waiting for results.

### B

**Backpressure**
The resistance or force opposing the desired flow of data through software.

**Batch Processing**
Processing data in groups rather than individually.

### C

**Cache**
Temporary storage of frequently accessed data for faster retrieval.

**Callback**
A function passed as an argument to be executed after an operation completes.

**CI/CD (Continuous Integration/Continuous Deployment)**
Automated processes for integrating code changes and deploying applications.

**Containerization**
Packaging applications with their dependencies for consistent deployment.

### D

**Daemon**
A background process that runs continuously.

**DataFrame**
A two-dimensional data structure with labeled axes (rows and columns).

**Dependency Injection**
A technique where objects receive their dependencies from external sources.

### E

**Endpoint**
A specific URL where an API can be accessed.

**Environment Variable**
A dynamic value that can affect the behavior of running processes.

**Exception Handling**
The process of responding to exceptional circumstances during program execution.

### F

**Factory Pattern**
A design pattern for creating objects without specifying their exact classes.

**Feature Engineering**
The process of creating new features from raw data for machine learning.

### G

**Garbage Collection**
Automatic memory management that reclaims memory occupied by unused objects.

**Git**
A distributed version control system for tracking changes in source code.

### H

**Hash Function**
A function that maps data of arbitrary size to fixed-size values.

**HTTP/HTTPS**
Protocols for transmitting data over the internet (Secure version includes encryption).

### I

**Idempotent**
An operation that produces the same result when applied multiple times.

**Immutable**
An object whose state cannot be modified after creation.

### J

**JSON (JavaScript Object Notation)**
A lightweight data-interchange format.

**JWT (JSON Web Token)**
A compact method for securely transmitting information between parties.

### K

**Kafka**
A distributed event streaming platform for high-performance data pipelines.

### L

**Lambda Function**
An anonymous function defined inline.

**Latency**
The time delay between cause and effect in a system.

**Load Balancing**
Distributing workload across multiple computing resources.

### M

**Microservices**
An architectural style structuring applications as collections of loosely coupled services.

**Middleware**
Software that acts as a bridge between an operating system and applications.

**MongoDB**
A NoSQL document-oriented database.

**Mutex**
A synchronization primitive that prevents simultaneous access to a shared resource.

### N

**NumPy**
A Python library for numerical computing with multi-dimensional arrays.

### O

**ORM (Object-Relational Mapping)**
A technique for converting data between incompatible type systems.

**Overfitting**
When a machine learning model learns training data too well, performing poorly on new data.

### P

**Pandas**
A Python library for data manipulation and analysis.

**Parallelization**
Executing multiple processes simultaneously.

**Pickle**
Python's serialization protocol for converting objects to byte streams.

**Polling**
Repeatedly checking for a condition at regular intervals.

### Q

**Query Optimization**
Improving database query performance through various techniques.

**Queue**
A data structure following First-In-First-Out (FIFO) principle.

### R

**Race Condition**
When a system's behavior depends on the sequence or timing of uncontrollable events.

**Redis**
An in-memory data structure store used as database, cache, and message broker.

**Regex (Regular Expression)**
A sequence of characters defining a search pattern.

**REST (Representational State Transfer)**
An architectural style for designing networked applications.

### S

**Serialization**
Converting an object into a format that can be stored or transmitted.

**Singleton Pattern**
A design pattern restricting class instantiation to a single instance.

**SSL/TLS**
Cryptographic protocols for secure communication over networks.

**Streaming**
Continuous transmission of data without requiring complete download.

### T

**Thread**
The smallest sequence of programmed instructions that can be managed independently.

**Throttling**
Limiting the rate of operations to prevent overload.

**Timestamp**
A sequence of characters identifying when an event occurred.

### U

**UDP**
User Datagram Protocol, a connectionless communication protocol.

**UUID (Universally Unique Identifier)**
A 128-bit number used to identify information in computer systems.

### V

**Vectorization**
Performing operations on entire arrays rather than individual elements.

**Virtual Environment**
An isolated Python environment with its own packages and dependencies.

### W

**WebSocket**
A protocol providing full-duplex communication channels over a single TCP connection.

**Webhook**
An HTTP callback triggered by specific events.

### Y

**YAML (Yet Another Markup Language)**
A human-readable data serialization standard.

## Machine Learning Terms

### A

**Accuracy**
The percentage of correct predictions made by a model.

**Activation Function**
A function that determines the output of a neural network node.

### B

**Bias**
The difference between a model's predictions and true values.

**Boosting**
An ensemble method that combines weak learners sequentially.

### C

**Classification**
Predicting discrete categories or classes.

**Clustering**
Grouping similar data points together.

**Confusion Matrix**
A table showing the performance of a classification model.

**Cross-Validation**
A technique for assessing model performance on different data subsets.

### D

**Decision Tree**
A tree-like model of decisions and their consequences.

**Deep Learning**
Machine learning using neural networks with multiple layers.

### E

**Ensemble Learning**
Combining multiple models to improve predictions.

**Epoch**
One complete pass through the training dataset.

### F

**F1 Score**
The harmonic mean of precision and recall.

**Feature**
An individual measurable property of data.

### G

**Gradient Boosting**
An ensemble technique building models sequentially to correct previous errors.

**Gradient Descent**
An optimization algorithm for finding minimum values.

### H

**Hyperparameter**
A parameter set before the learning process begins.

### K

**K-Fold Cross-Validation**
Dividing data into k subsets for validation.

### L

**Learning Rate**
The step size for updating model parameters during training.

**Logistic Regression**
A classification algorithm for binary outcomes.

### N

**Neural Network**
A computing system inspired by biological neural networks.

**Normalization**
Scaling features to a standard range.

### O

**One-Hot Encoding**
Converting categorical variables into binary vectors.

**Outlier**
A data point significantly different from other observations.

### P

**Precision**
The ratio of true positives to all positive predictions.

**Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**
A dimensionality reduction technique.

### R

**Random Forest**
An ensemble of decision trees using bagging.

**Recall**
The ratio of true positives to all actual positives.

**Regression**
Predicting continuous numerical values.

**Regularization**
Techniques to prevent overfitting by penalizing complexity.

**ROC Curve**
Receiver Operating Characteristic curve showing true vs false positive rates.

### S

**Supervised Learning**
Learning from labeled training data.

**Support Vector Machine (SVM)**
A classification algorithm finding optimal separating hyperplanes.

### T

**Training Set**
Data used to train a machine learning model.

**Test Set**
Data used to evaluate final model performance.

### U

**Underfitting**
When a model is too simple to capture data patterns.

**Unsupervised Learning**
Learning patterns from unlabeled data.

### V

**Validation Set**
Data used to tune model hyperparameters.
